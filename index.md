# 👤 About Me

I am a Ph.D. scholar at the **Centre for Linguistic Science and Technology, Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati**, working at the intersection of **Natural Language Processing** (NLP) and **Computer Vision** (CV), i.e., **Vision-Language Understanding** (VLU).

My research primarily focuses on developing **Automatic Image Captioning systems for low-resource languages**, with a special emphasis on the Assamese language. While mainstream AI technologies are often optimized for resource-rich languages like English, my work seeks to bridge this gap by designing models that are linguistically aware, data-efficient, and culturally inclusive.

Over the course of my doctoral work, I have:

- Introduced novel architectures that integrate semantic priors and spatially encoded transformer models for generating linguistically accurate and contextually meaningful captions.

- Demonstrated how language-centric datasets and customized training strategies outperform generic transfer-learning approaches.

- Trained and released AssameseGPT2, a GPT-2 model built from scratch on IndicCorpV2, and extended it into multimodal captioning systems (AsCap-GPT2).

I have collaborated on multiple projects in LLM training, multimodal AI, and dataset creation. Most recently, I worked as an AI Research Intern at **Accenture AI Labs**, improving small Vision-Language Models for chart understanding, and I am currently a Guest Faculty at **NIT Meghalaya**. I have also served as a faculty resource person and industry trainer, delivering sessions on Generative AI, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning for academic institutions and professional training programs.

My long-term vision is to build scalable, inclusive AI frameworks that not only advance research but also create meaningful real-world impact by enabling AI for everyone, regardless of language or resource availability.

# 📘 Ph.D. Thesis

**Automatic Image Caption Synthesis for Low-Resource Assamese Language**

Automatic Image Captioning (AIC) research has largely focused on resource-rich languages such as English, while morphologically rich Indian languages like Assamese, spoken by over 15 million people, remain underserved due to scarce data and the lack of dedicated models. This thesis establishes the first complete framework for Assamese image captioning:

- **Language-specific training:** Showed that training directly on Assamese captions produces more fluent and accurate captions than translating English model outputs, motivating native Assamese datasets.
- **Datasets:** Introduced three large-scale Assamese caption datasets: COCO-AC and Flickr30K-AC (translated with manual curation) and ChitraLekha10K (style transfer and native-speaker annotation).
- **Language-specific models:** Proposed VSSA, SPG-TFR, and VSB-TFR, which use semantic attributes, semantic priors, and bilinear attention to capture Assamese inflections. VSB-TFR achieves the best performance.
- **Pretrained decoders:** Developed AsCap-GPT2, which couples visual features with a pretrained Assamese GPT-2 via cross-attention and performs strongly in few-shot and full-data settings.
- **Efficient models:** Designed AC-Lite and VSB-TFR-Lite for resource-constrained devices, achieving comparable performance at a fraction of the compute.

# 💡  Research Interests

My research focuses on the intersection of **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** and **Computer Vision (CV)**, i.e., **Vision–Language Understanding (VLU)**, with an emphasis on **low-resource Indian languages**. 

My doctoral thesis focuses on **Automatic Image Caption generation for the low-resource Assamese language**. Automatic image caption generation is the task of producing natural language descriptions of images. My primary research goal is to extend this process to **low-resource languages**, where models must not only understand visual content but also generate semantically and syntactically accurate descriptions in a linguistically rich setting. Through my work on image captioning, I aim to design computationally efficient multimodal AI systems that are linguistically aware and culturally relevant.

**Key areas of interest**
- Vision–language models for low-resource Indian languages.
- Lightweight and efficient multimodal models for edge devices.  
- Large Language Models (LLMs) adapted for Indian languages.  
- Applications of multimodal AI for accessibility and cultural heritage.
